# USB devices

Avocado Desktop provisions inside a VM on macOS and in containers on Linux. A board you plug into your computer only reaches them if Desktop passes it through, and Desktop only passes through USB devices on its **allow list**.

Known devkits are on the allow list already. **Any other hardware needs enabling the first time you provision it**, including your own carrier boards and modules. Until you enable it, `avocado provision` waits for a device that never shows up.

## Enable your hardware

1. Put the board in the mode its provisioning step asks for, such as recovery or serial download, and connect it to your computer.
2. In Desktop, open **Devices** in the sidebar. The **USB devices** pane lists every USB device your computer can see. Hubs and internal devices are hidden.
3. Find the board's row and turn its toggle on.

The row goes from `available` to `attaching`, then `attached`. Provision once it shows `attached`.

Desktop remembers the devices you enable, so you only do this once for each device. The list lives in `~/Library/Application Support/Avocado/usb_allowlist.json` on macOS and `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/avocado/usb_allowlist.json` on Linux.

### Hardware that changes USB ID while flashing

Some hardware comes back as a different USB device partway through a flash. For example, a boot ROM hands over to a boot loader, which then starts a flashing gadget. Each of those devices needs enabling.

Those later devices only exist while a flash is running, so you can't toggle them from the list ahead of time. You can handle them in either of two ways:

- **Enable them as they appear.** Start the flash. When it stalls, open **Devices**, turn on the new row, and provision again. The **Events** pane shows each device as it appears.
- **Add them ahead of time.** If you know the VID:PID of each stage, enter them in the **Add to allow list by VID:PID** card and click **Add**.

## Device states

| State         | Meaning                                                                         |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `available`   | Plugged in and staying on your computer. Turn its toggle on to pass it through. |
| `attaching`   | Enabled, and being passed through to the VM.                                    |
| `attached`    | In the VM, and visible to `avocado provision`.                                  |
| `detaching`   | Being returned to your computer.                                                |
| `gone`        | Unplugged, or re-enumerating as another USB device.                             |
| `error`       | The pass-through failed. The row shows the error.                               |
| `not present` | Enabled, but not plugged in.                                                    |

If a device sits in `attaching` for 60 seconds, the attach times out with an error. Click **↻** on the row to try again.

### The generic Linux gadget stays on your computer

A running Linux board often shows up as `1d6b:0104`, the generic Linux gadget, which carries its USB network and serial console. Desktop only passes that device to the VM while a flash that needs it is running. The rest of the time it stays on your computer, even when enabled, and its row says so. To pass it through anyway, click **Attach** on the row.

## Requirements

**macOS.** Desktop's USB helper ships with the app, and macOS asks you to approve it once. Until you do, every page shows **Approve USB passthrough to flash devices**. Click **Open Login Items** and allow Avocado there. If your hardware appears as a USB drive during a flash, Desktop also needs the **Removable Volumes** permission. macOS asks for it the first time: "Avocado.app would like to access files on a removable volume". If you denied or dismissed that prompt, turn on **Removable Volumes** for Avocado under **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Files and Folders**.

**Linux.** Pass-through uses USB/IP, which needs `pkexec` and your distribution's `usbip` tools. Installing the `.deb` gets both, unless you used `--no-install-recommends`. On Ubuntu, the `usbip` tools come in `linux-tools-$(uname -r)`; check that `usbip version` runs cleanly. If something is missing, the **USB devices** pane tells you what to install.
